Exam Focus — What the Finals Actually Test

Frequency analysis of six IE2106 final papers (AY2023/24 – AY2025/26) and what to prioritise

📡 What the finals are really testing
  • Total marks in the 50-part exam bank: 600 across 6 finals. The two heaviest areas are PDE (135) and confidence intervals/hypothesis tests (96), joined by ODE-with-Laplace (96). Numerical methods (80) appear in all 6 papers.
  • Quiz (Tutorials 8–11): the quiz covers numerical methods (Tut 8–9) and Laplace (Tut 10–11). These are also over a third of the final's marks, so quiz prep = final prep. Do the Quiz Drill and Weeks 7–10 first.
  • Marks counted are the parts that I could cleanly attribute to a topic (Q1(a) type stats parts, Q2–Q4 etc.); total marks per paper are 100, so the table is a guide to proportions rather than exact weights.

🎯 What to focus on (in order)

  1. Laplace ODE routine (Wk 10): the same 10–15 mark question in every paper. Practise until you never lose a sign.
  2. Hypothesis tests + “find the range” (page): Question 1(b) in all 6 papers; t-test, χ² and the backwards-range twist.
  3. PDE direct integration, classification, separation of variables (Wk 12, Wk 13): predictable template, ~25 marks per paper.
  4. Numerical methods (Wk 7, Wk 8): Newton divided differences + error bound, fixed-point iteration + stopping criterion, Heun/improved Euler.
  5. Transfer function and pulse inputs (Wk 11): delayed steps, H(s)X(s), partial fractions.
  6. pdf/CDF/E/Var, binomial/Poisson, normal: reliable marks that every paper opens with.

🔀 Does Semester 2 have more Laplace?

Marks on Laplace + ODE/transfer-function questions per paper: Sem 1 papers = 25, 25, 15 (mean 21.7); Sem 2 papers = 25, 25, 30 (mean 26.7).

Verdict: slightly more in Sem 2, but weakly. 2025-26 Sem 2 stands out (30 marks Laplace, only 10 marks PDE) whereas 2025-26 Sem 1 gave just 15. Every other pair is nearly identical (25 marks). With only three papers per semester this is weak evidence, so don't gamble: you still get a full 25 marks of Laplace in most papers, and since your cohort does Laplace after stats you should expect the usual ~25 ± 10. The practical consequence is simple: do not skip Laplace to save time for PDE.

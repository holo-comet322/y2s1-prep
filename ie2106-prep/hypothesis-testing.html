Hypothesis Testing: z, t and χ² Tests

Statistics slides sections 9–11 · Question 1(b) in every final

📡 Exam radar — Hypothesis testing
  • Hypothesis testing is Question 1(b) in ALL 6 finals (8–18 marks each, the biggest recurring item in the stats half). Mean test with unknown σ (t-test) and variance test (χ²) are equally likely; a z-test with known σ appears as a smaller part.
  • The signature twist (5 of 6 finals): “What range of α (or μ0, or sample mean) gives the same / opposite conclusion?” This is just the test run backwards — see the ranges section.
  • “State assumptions” is worth marks: normal population (or n > 30 for the mean by CLT; χ² needs normality regardless) and a random sample.

Concept 1 of 5

The same 4 steps every time

  1. Hypotheses. H0 is the boring “nothing changed” claim with an equals sign (μ = μ0). H1 is what you want to show: ≠ (two-tail), < (lower-tail) or > (upper-tail). The words in the question decide: “different/changed” → ≠; “reduced/shorter/below” → <; “increased/above” → >.
  2. Test statistic (z, t or χ²) computed from the sample.
  3. Critical value / rejection region from the table at level α (split α/2 into each tail for two-tail).
  4. Decision in words about H1: “reject H0: there is sufficient evidence (at α) that …” or “fail to reject: insufficient evidence that …”. Never say “accept H0 is true”.
AnalogyA courtroom: H0 = “innocent”. You only convict (reject H0) if the evidence is stronger than a pre-set bar (α). Not enough evidence ≠ proven innocent.
Concept 2 of 5

Test for the mean, σ known (z-test)

Z = (X̄ − μ0) / (σ/√n)
X̄sample mean.
μ0the value claimed in H0.
σ/√nstandard error: the typical wobble of a sample mean.
Zhow many standard errors the sample mean is from the claim.

Needs X normal, or n > 30 (CLT). Reject if Z falls beyond ±zα/2 (two-tail), below −zα (lower) or above zα (upper).

Lecture Example 10.1 — new checking procedure, α = 1.5%
  1. H0: μ = 5; H1: μ < 5 (“shorten”). n = 36 > 30 so CLT applies.
  2. Z = (4.85 − 5)/(0.5/√36) = −0.15/0.0833 = −1.80.
  3. Critical value: z0.015 = −2.17. Since −1.80 > −2.17, we fail to reject H0: not enough evidence at 1.5% that the new procedure reduces the mean time. (p = 0.036 > 0.015.)
Concept 3 of 5

Test for the mean, σ unknown (t-test)

T = (X̄ − μ0) / (S/√n),   df = n − 1

Same as the z-test but the sample standard deviation S replaces σ, so extra uncertainty is paid for by the fatter t-distribution (df = n−1). Needs X approximately normal (or large n). Use the t-table row n−1 and column α (one-tail) or α/2 (two-tail).

2023-24 S1 Q1(b)(ii)(a): n = 31, x̄ = 148, s = 5, μ0 = 150, α = 5%, two-tail
  1. H0: μ = 150; H1: μ ≠ 150.
  2. T = (148−150)/(5/√31) = −2/0.898 = −2.227, df = 30.
  3. t0.025,30 = 2.042. |T| = 2.227 > 2.042 ⇒ reject H0: the historical mean 150 g no longer seems to hold (p ≈ 0.034).
Concept 4 of 5

Test for the variance (χ² test)

χ² = (n−1)S² / σ0²,   df = n − 1
S²sample variance (square the sample standard deviation!).
σ0²variance claimed in H0 (square the claimed standard deviation).
χ²ratio of the sample's spread to the claimed spread, scaled by df. About df if H0 is true.

The χ² distribution is not symmetric, so the two critical values of a two-tail test are different: χ²1−α/2 (left) and χ²α/2 (right). Lower-tail test: reject if χ² < χ²1−α; upper-tail: reject if χ² > χ²α. Table columns run 0.99, 0.975, 0.95, 0.90, 0.5, 0.1, 0.05, 0.025, 0.01 — read the column for the area to the right.

Lecture Example 11.1 — gold plating, n = 101, s = 0.98, σ0 = 1.2, α = 2.5%
  1. H0: σ² = 1.44; H1: σ² < 1.44 (lower tail).
  2. χ² = 100(0.98)²/1.44 = 66.69, df = 100.
  3. Critical value: χ²0.975,100 = 74.22. 66.69 < 74.22 ⇒ reject H0: the new procedure reduces variability (p ≈ 0.004).
2024-25 S2 Q1(b)(ii): n = 61, s = 12, σ0 = 10, upper tail, α = 5%
  1. χ² = 60(144)/100 = 86.4, df = 60.
  2. χ²0.05,60 = 79.08 < 86.4 ⇒ reject: the standard deviation has increased (p ≈ 0.014).
Concept 5 of 5

“What range of α / μ0 / sample mean gives the same conclusion?”

These twists appear in 5 of 6 finals. They are the same test run backwards: fix the test statistic and find where the critical value would have to sit, or fix the critical value and move the other quantity.

Asked forWhat to do
range of αThe test statistic is fixed. Decision is “reject” when the critical value is less extreme than the statistic. Find the table entries on either side of your statistic; α larger than that column's value rejects, smaller does not. (E.g. ht1: T = −2.227 lies between t0.025,30 = 2.042 and t0.01,30 = 2.457 ⇒ two-tail α between 2×0.01 = 0.02 and 2×0.025 = 0.05; exactly, α ≥ p = 0.034 rejects.)
range of μ0 (or σ0, d*, f0)Fix α, so the critical value is fixed. Replace μ0 by an unknown in the statistic and solve the inequality. (ht1: reject when |148 − μ0| > 2.042·5/√31 = 1.834, i.e. μ0 < 146.17 or μ0 > 149.83.)
range of sample mean / x̄Use the critical value to define the rejection region in terms of x̄: x̄ = μ0 ± z·σ/√n, then pick the side(s) asked for.
AnalogyThe test is a high-jump bar at height = critical value. “Find the range of α” means moving the bar up and down to see where your jumper (the statistic) just clears it.

Final 2023-24 Sem 1 · Q1(b)(ii) · 9 marks
Tests: Hypothesis testing (Wk 6 part 2) · 'Find the range of α / μ0' questions  |  see it in the exam bank
t-test, two-tailrange of αrange of μ0

Same waffles (n = 31, x̄ = 148, s = 5). Historic mean μ0 = 150 g. (a) At α = 5%, can we conclude the historical mean still holds? State assumptions. (b) To reach the same conclusion with μ0 = 150, what range of α is allowed? (c) With α fixed at 5%, what range of μ0 gives the same conclusion?

Try it yourself first, then reveal the solution
Solution

What it's asking: a normal two-tail t-test, then two “reverse-engineering” questions: first move α, then move μ0, and ask where the decision stays the same.

  1. Assumption & set-up: weights approx. normal (σ unknown, n=31 ⇒ t with 30 d.f.). H0: μ = 150, H1: μ ≠ 150 (two-tail).
  2. Test statistic: t = (148 − 150)/(5/√31) = −2/0.8980 = −2.227.
  3. Critical values: t0.025 (30 d.f.) = 2.042 ⇒ reject if |t| > 2.042. Here 2.227 > 2.042 ⇒ reject H0: the historical mean of 150 g no longer seems to hold.
  4. (b) Range of α (same conclusion = still reject): reject whenever |t| exceeds tα/2. Table: t0.025 = 2.042 < 2.227 < t0.01 = 2.457, so the boundary α lies between 2% and 5% — the exact boundary (p-value) is α ≈ 0.0336. So we reject for α > about 0.034 (any α ≥ 5% works; α = 2% would flip the decision).
  5. (c) Range of μ0 (α = 5%): reject when |x̄ − μ0| > 2.042(5/√31) = 1.834. So μ0 < 148 − 1.834 = 146.17 or μ0 > 148 + 1.834 = 149.83. (150 is in the second region ✓ consistent.)

This “find the range” pattern appears in every final: solve test statistic = critical value for the unknown (see the dedicated section on the hypothesis-testing page).

Final 2023-24 Sem 2 · Q1(b) · 12 marks
Tests: Hypothesis testing (Wk 6 part 2) · 'Find the range of α / μ0' questions  |  see it in the exam bank
chi-square test on σ, two-tailnew standard σ0 to reverse

Mooncake standard: diameter 8 cm with standard deviation 0.3 cm. A new mould: n = 51, x̄ = 8.01, s = 0.25. At α = 5%, can we say the standard deviation differs from 0.3 cm? State assumptions. To reverse the earlier conclusion, what should the new production standard for the standard deviation be?

Try it yourself first, then reveal the solution
Solution
  1. Assumption: diameters are approx. normal. H0: σ = 0.3 (σ² = 0.09), H1: σ ≠ 0.3 (two-tail), α = 0.05.
  2. Statistic: χ² = (n−1)s²/σ0² = 50(0.0625)/0.09 = 34.72, d.f. = 50.
  3. Critical values (table, 50 d.f.): lower χ²0.975 = 32.35, upper χ²0.025 = 71.42. Since 32.35 < 34.72 < 71.42 it is inside the accept region ⇒ do not reject H0: not enough evidence that the standard deviation differs from 0.3 cm (the exact p-value is about 0.099).
  4. Reverse the conclusion (i.e. reject): the statistic becomes 3.125/σ0² and must fall outside [32.35, 71.42]. • 3.125/σ0² < 32.35 ⇒ σ0² > 0.09660 ⇒ σ0 > 0.311 cm. • 3.125/σ0² > 71.42 ⇒ σ0² < 0.04376 ⇒ σ0 < 0.209 cm.

Careful: σ0 sits in the denominator, so the inequalities flip when you solve for it.

Final 2024-25 Sem 1 · Q1(b) · 16 marks
Tests: Hypothesis testing (Wk 6 part 2)  |  see it in the exam bank
two tests: t-test on μ and χ² one-tail on σadvice

A bakery's standards: (S1) mean filling weight 25 g; (S2) standard deviation of filling weights below 2 g. Sample of 41 curry puffs: mean 24.7 g, standard deviation 1.9 g. Test both standards at the 5% level. State assumptions. How would you advise the bakery?

Try it yourself first, then reveal the solution
Solution

What it's asking: two separate tests on the same data. Decide each H1 from the wording: S1 says the mean “should be” 25 → two-tail (any departure matters); S2 says the s.d. should be “below 2” → H1: σ < 2 (the thing we want evidence for).

  1. Assumption (both tests): filling weights approx. normal (n = 41 ≥ 30 also lets the CLT cover the mean test).
  2. S1: H0: μ = 25; H1: μ ≠ 25. t = (24.7 − 25)/(1.9/√41) = −0.3/0.2967 = −1.011. Critical: ±t0.025 (40 d.f.) = ±2.021. |−1.011| < 2.021 ⇒ do not reject: the mean standard S1 is upheld (no evidence it is off 25 g).
  3. S2: H0: σ² = 4; H1: σ² < 4 (lower-tail). χ² = 40(1.9²)/4 = 40(3.61)/4 = 36.1. Critical (left tail, 40 d.f.): χ²0.95 = 26.51. Reject only if χ² < 26.51; 36.1 is bigger ⇒ do not reject H0: no significant evidence that σ is below 2 g, so S2 is not demonstrated.
  4. Advice: the average filling is on target, but the sample s = 1.9 g is too close to 2 g to prove the variability standard is met. Reduce variability (better dosing/filling-machine calibration, consistent filling procedure) and/or test a larger sample — a larger n makes the same s more convincing.

Final 2024-25 Sem 2 · Q1(b)(ii) · 8 marks
Tests: Hypothesis testing (Wk 6 part 2) · 'Find the range of α / μ0' questions  |  see it in the exam bank
chi-square test on σ, upper-tailrange of α to reverse

Standard deviation of monthly rainfall in the 1950s was 10 mm. From the same sample (n = 61, s = 12 mm), can we conclude the standard deviation has increased? Test at α = 5%. State an assumption. What range of α reverses the conclusion?

Try it yourself first, then reveal the solution
Solution
  1. Assumption: monthly rainfall approx. normal. H0: σ² = 100; H1: σ² > 100 (“increased” → upper-tail).
  2. χ² = 60(144)/100 = 86.4 (60 d.f.). Critical value χ²0.05 = 79.08.
  3. 86.4 > 79.08 ⇒ reject H0: significant evidence that the s.d. of monthly rainfall has increased.
  4. Reverse it: we must stop rejecting, so the critical value must be at least 86.4. Table (60 d.f.): χ²0.025 = 83.30 (still rejects), χ²0.01 = 88.38 (> 86.4, no longer rejects). The exact p-value is 0.0144, so any α ≤ about 0.014 (certainly α ≤ 1%) reverses the conclusion.

Final 2025-26 Sem 1 · Q1(b) · 10 marks
Tests: Hypothesis testing (Wk 6 part 2) · 'Find the range of α / μ0' questions  |  see it in the exam bank
t-test, lower-tailrange of standard d*

A machine is set to make fish balls of standard diameter d* = 2.0 cm. A slightly smaller actual diameter improves profit. Sample of 41: mean 1.99 cm, standard deviation 0.02 cm. At α = 5%, can the factory conclude the machine improves the profit margin? What range of d* keeps the same conclusion?

Try it yourself first, then reveal the solution
Solution
  1. Set-up: improvement = actual diameter smaller than the standard ⇒ H0: μ = 2.0; H1: μ < 2.0 (lower-tail). σ unknown, n = 41 (≥ 30, CLT) ⇒ t with 40 d.f.
  2. t = (1.99 − 2.00)/(0.02/√41) = −0.01/0.0031235 = −3.20.
  3. Critical value: −t0.05 (40 d.f.) = −1.684. −3.20 < −1.684 ⇒ reject H0: yes, the factory can conclude the machine improves the profit margin.
  4. Range of d* (same conclusion = still reject): need (1.99 − d*)/0.0031235 < −1.684 ⇒ 1.99 − d* < −0.005260 ⇒ d* > 1.9953 cm.

Final 2025-26 Sem 2 · Q1(b),(c) · 18 marks
Tests: Hypothesis testing (Wk 6 part 2) · 'Find the range of α / μ0' questions  |  see it in the exam bank
t-test upper-tailrange of μ0 / f0z-test with known σ: range of sample mean

(b) Hybrid enthusiasts want average fuel consumption above f0 = 32.5 km/L. From the sample (n = 30, mean 33, s = 0.6), can the maker conclude at 5% that Icebird is a hit? State assumptions. (ii) What range of f0 keeps the conclusion? (c) Now σ = 0.63 is known, n = 30, f0 = 33.1, α = 2.5%. What range of sample mean should they target?

Try it yourself first, then reveal the solution
Solution
  1. (b)(i) H0: μ = 32.5; H1: μ > 32.5. σ unknown, n = 30: assume approx. normal; t, 29 d.f. t = (33 − 32.5)/(0.6/√30) = 0.5/0.10954 = 4.564. Critical t0.05 (29 d.f.) = 1.699. 4.564 > 1.699 ⇒ reject H0: the manufacturer can conclude Icebird beats 32.5 km/L (a hit).
  2. (b)(ii) Still reject if (33 − f0)/0.10954 > 1.699 ⇒ 33 − f0 > 0.18613 ⇒ f0 < 32.814 km/L.
  3. (c) σ is known so use z: H0: μ = 33.1; H1: μ > 33.1; α = 0.025 ⇒ z0.025 = 1.96. Standard error = 0.63/√30 = 0.11502. Reject if x̄ > 33.1 + 1.96(0.11502) = 33.1 + 0.2254 ⇒ target x̄ > 33.325 km/L.

Note (c) asks for a range of the sample mean (the critical value of x̄), not a test — you simply convert the critical z back into x̄ units: x̄crit = μ0 + zασ/√n.

✍️ Write This Down

Copy these into your notes:

  • Steps: H0 (=) & H1 (≠, <, >) → statistic → critical value → decision in words about H1.
  • Z = (x̄−μ0)/(σ/√n); T = (x̄−μ0)/(s/√n), df = n−1; χ² = (n−1)s²/σ0², df = n−1.
  • Two-tail: use α/2 in each tail. χ² critical values are asymmetric.
  • Assumptions: random sample; normal population (or n > 30 for mean tests); χ² always needs normality.
  • Range questions: solve the inequality |statistic| > critical for the unknown (α, μ0 or x̄).

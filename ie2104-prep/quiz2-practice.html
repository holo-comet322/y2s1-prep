📘 Week 4

Q1 · Week 4

Simplify a + a′b.

  • A. a
  • B. a + b
  • C. ab
  • D. a′b
Reveal answer
Answer: B

Absorption variant: a + a′b = (a+a′)(a+b) = a + b (distributive law the other way round).

Q2 · Week 4

Which expression equals (a + b′)′ ?

  • A. a′ + b
  • B. a′b′
  • C. a′b
  • D. ab′
Reveal answer
Answer: C

De Morgan: (x+y)′ = x′y′, and (b′)′ = b, so (a+b′)′ = a′b.

Q3 · Week 4

Minterm m5 of f(a,b,c) (a = MSB) is:

  • A. a′bc
  • B. ab′c′
  • C. a + b′ + c
  • D. ab′c
Reveal answer
Answer: D

5 = 101 in binary: a = 1 (plain), b = 0 (complemented), c = 1 (plain) ⇒ ab′c. A minterm is an AND that is 1 for exactly one row.

Q4 · Week 4

Maxterm M3 of f(a,b,c) (a = MSB) is:

  • A. a + b + c
  • B. a′ + b + c
  • C. a + b′ + c
  • D. a + b′ + c′
Reveal answer
Answer: D

3 = 011. A maxterm is an OR that is 0 for exactly that row, so use the plain variable where the bit is 0 and the complement where it is 1: a + b′ + c′.

Q5 · Week 4

The consensus theorem says ab + a′c + bc equals:

  • A. ab + bc
  • B. ab + a′c
  • C. a′c + bc
  • D. ab + a′c + c
Reveal answer
Answer: B

The ‘consensus’ term bc is redundant: whenever bc = 1, either a = 1 (so ab covers it) or a = 0 (so a′c covers it).

Q6 · Week 4

x ⊕ y (XOR) equals:

  • A. xy + x′y′
  • B. (x + y)′
  • C. x′y + xy′
  • D. x + y
Reveal answer
Answer: C

XOR is 1 when exactly one input is 1: x′y + xy′. The first option is XNOR.

Q7 · Week 4

A two-level NAND-NAND circuit directly realises a function in which form?

  • A. Product of sums
  • B. Sum of products
  • C. Exclusive-OR form
  • D. Only functions with a single minterm
Reveal answer
Answer: B

NAND(NAND(inputs)) applies De Morgan twice: (x′′·y′′)… = sum of products. NOR-NOR gives product of sums.

Q8 · Week 4

The dual of a(b + c′) is (swap AND ↔ OR and 0 ↔ 1, keep the variables):

  • A. a′ + b′c
  • B. a + bc
  • C. a(b′ + c)
  • D. a + bc′
Reveal answer
Answer: D

Swap the operators only: a·(b + c′) becomes a + (b·c′). Complements are NOT touched; that is what separates a dual from a complement.

📘 Week 5

Q9 · Week 5

Minimal SOP of y(a,b,c,d) = Σm(0,1,2,3,7,8,9,10,11,15) (the 2025-26 S2 Q1(b) function):

  • A. b′ + cd
  • B. a′b′ + cd
  • C. b′ + c′d
  • D. b′d + cd
Reveal answer
Answer: A

All eight cells with b = 0 (0,1,2,3,8,9,10,11) form one group of 8 ⇒ b′. The remaining 1s, 7 and 15, join 3 and 11 to form the group of four with c = d = 1, i.e. cd. Result b′ + cd (verified with a solver).

Q10 · Week 5

Minimal SOP of f(a,b,c,d) = Σm(0,2,5,7,8,10,13,15) is:

  • A. b′d′ + bd
  • B. b′d + bd′
  • C. a′d′ + bd
  • D. c′d′ + cd
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Two groups of four: the four corners-and-edges cells (0,2,8,10) give b′d′; the centre block (5,7,13,15) gives bd. This is XNOR(b,d).

Q11 · Week 5

In a K-map, a group of 4 adjacent 1s in a 3-variable map eliminates how many variables?

  • A. 0
  • B. 1
  • C. 2
  • D. 3
Reveal answer
Answer: C

A group of 2k cells eliminates k variables: 4 = 2² ⇒ two variables vanish, leaving one literal.

Q12 · Week 5

f(a,b,c) = Σm(1,3,7) + d(5) (d = don’t care). The minimal SOP is:

  • A. c
  • B. a′c + bc
  • C. a′b′c + bc
  • D. ac + a′c
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Treat the don’t-care at 5 (101) as a 1: cells 1,3,5,7 are every cell with c = 1, a group of four ⇒ f = c. Don’t-cares are used only when they enlarge a group.

Q13 · Week 5

f(a,b,c) = Σm(0,2,4,5,6). The minimal POS is:

  • A. (a + c′)(b′ + c′)
  • B. (a′ + c)(b + c)
  • C. (a + c)(b′ + c′)
  • D. a′c + bc
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Group the 0s (cells 1,3,7): pair (1,3) = a′c-type and pair (3,7) = bc-type. f′ = a′c + bc, so f = (a+c′)(b′+c′) by De Morgan.

Q14 · Week 5

The K-map columns for variables cd are labelled in the order 00, 01, 11, 10. This is Gray code because:

  • A. adjacent columns differ in exactly one bit
  • B. the numbers are in increasing binary order
  • C. it makes the map smaller
  • D. it is needed for don’t cares
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Adjacent cells must differ in one variable so that neighbouring 1s can be combined by the rule xy + xy′ = x. Binary order (00,01,10,11) would break that.

📘 Week 6

Q15 · Week 6

A 3-to-8 decoder (active-high outputs) is used to realise F = Σm(1,2,4,7). What extra gate is needed?

  • A. A 4-input OR gate
  • B. A 4-input AND gate
  • C. Nothing
  • D. A 3-input NOR gate
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Each output yi is minterm mi; OR together the outputs that are 1 in F: m1 + m2 + m4 + m7, a 4-input OR. (If the decoder were active-low, use a NAND.)

Q16 · Week 6

f(a,b,c) = Σm(0,3,5,6) is built on a 4:1 MUX with select lines S1 = a, S0 = b. The data inputs (D0, D1, D2, D3) must be:

  • A. (c′, c, c, c′)
  • B. (c, c′, c′, c)
  • C. (0, 1, 1, 0)
  • D. (c′, c′, c, c)
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Fix a,b and read f as a function of c: ab=00 → only m0 (c=0) ⇒ c′; 01 → m3 (c=1) ⇒ c; 10 → m5 (c=1) ⇒ c; 11 → m6 (c=0) ⇒ c′. (Checked row by row by program.)

Q17 · Week 6

How many select lines does an 8-to-1 multiplexer have?

  • A. 2
  • B. 3
  • C. 4
  • D. 8
Reveal answer
Answer: B

n select lines choose among 2n inputs; 8 = 2³.

Q18 · Week 6

Which statement about PLA and PAL is correct?

  • A. In a PLA both the AND and OR planes are programmable; in a PAL only the AND plane is
  • B. In a PAL both planes are programmable; in a PLA only the OR plane is
  • C. Both have a fixed AND plane
  • D. A PAL stores bits like a RAM
Reveal answer
Answer: A

PLA = programmable AND + programmable OR (can share product terms). PAL = programmable AND + fixed OR (cheaper, less flexible).

Q19 · Week 6

A 4-input priority encoder (highest-priority input = I3) has I2 = 1 and I1 = 1, all others 0. The output code is:

  • A. 01
  • B. 10
  • C. 11
  • D. 00
Reveal answer
Answer: B

The highest-priority active input is I2, so the output is its index, 2 = 10. I1 is ignored.

Q20 · Week 6

Full adder inputs a = 1, b = 1, Cin = 1. The outputs (S, Cout) are:

  • A. (1, 1)
  • B. (0, 1)
  • C. (1, 0)
  • D. (0, 0)
Reveal answer
Answer: A

1+1+1 = 3 = 112: sum bit 1, carry 1. S = a⊕b⊕Cin = 1; Cout = ab + Cin(a⊕b) = 1 + 0 = 1.

📘 Week 7

Q21 · Week 7

A 4-bit ripple-carry adder's worst-case delay grows with the number of bits n because:

  • A. the carry must propagate through every stage in turn
  • B. every stage needs a separate clock
  • C. the sum bits are computed serially in time
  • D. the adder must be reset between bits
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Each full adder waits for the carry from the previous one, so delay ∝ n. Carry-look-ahead computes carries in parallel from P and G.

Q22 · Week 7

For one bit position of a carry-look-ahead adder, with a = 1 and b = 1: the generate and propagate signals G = ab and P = a⊕b are:

  • A. G = 1, P = 0
  • B. G = 0, P = 1
  • C. G = 1, P = 1
  • D. G = 0, P = 0
Reveal answer
Answer: A

G = 1·1 = 1 (this stage creates a carry by itself); P = 1⊕1 = 0 (it does not merely pass one along).

Q23 · Week 7

Comparing A = 1010 and B = 1001 from the MSB down, the comparator outputs (A>B, A=B, A<B) are:

  • A. (1, 0, 0)
  • B. (0, 1, 0)
  • C. (0, 0, 1)
  • D. (1, 1, 0)
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Bits 3 and 2 match (1,0); at bit 1 A has 1 and B has 0, so A > B (10 > 9). Once a difference is found the lower bits don’t matter.

Q24 · Week 7

When cascading two 4-bit 7485 comparators for an 8-bit comparison, the cascade inputs (A>B, A=B, A<B) of the higher-order chip are connected to:

  • A. the outputs of the lower-order chip
  • B. logic 1 on all three
  • C. the inputs A and B directly
  • D. ground on all three
Reveal answer
Answer: A

The lower chip decides if the top 4 bits are equal; its result must be passed up. (The lowest chip itself gets A=B tied to 1, others 0.)

📘 Week 8

Q25 · Week 8

A cross-coupled NOR SR latch has S = R = 1. The outputs (Q, Q′) are:

  • A. Q = 0 and Q′ = 0 (an invalid, forbidden state)
  • B. Q = 1 and Q′ = 0
  • C. Q = 0 and Q′ = 1
  • D. Unchanged (memory state)
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Both NOR gates see a 1 and output 0. Both outputs are 0, which breaks Q′ = complement of Q and is unpredictable when S and R return to 0 together.

Q26 · Week 8

A T flip-flop currently stores Q = 1. With T = 1 at the next active clock edge, Q becomes:

  • A. 0
  • B. 1
  • C. unchanged
  • D. undefined
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Characteristic equation Q+ = T ⊕ Q = 1 ⊕ 1 = 0: T = 1 toggles.

Q27 · Week 8

The characteristic equation of the JK flip-flop is:

  • A. Q+ = JQ′ + K′Q
  • B. Q+ = J + KQ
  • C. Q+ = JK
  • D. Q+ = J ⊕ K
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Check: J=K=0 → Q (hold); J=1,K=0 → 1; J=0,K=1 → 0; J=K=1 → Q′ (toggle).

Q28 · Week 8

The characteristic equation of the SR flip-flop (S·R = 0) is:

  • A. Q+ = S + R′Q
  • B. Q+ = S′ + RQ
  • C. Q+ = SR
  • D. Q+ = S ⊕ Q
Reveal answer
Answer: A

S sets, R resets, otherwise hold: Q+ = S + R′Q.

Q29 · Week 8

To make a flip-flop go from Q = 0 to Q+ = 1, which inputs are needed?

  • A. D = 1; T = 1; J = 1 with K = x
  • B. D = 0; T = 1; J = x with K = 1
  • C. D = 1; T = 0; J = 0 with K = x
  • D. D = 0; T = 0; J = 1 with K = 0 only
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Excitation table: D just equals the next state (1). T must toggle (1). JK: J = 1, K = don’t-care (works for both set and toggle).

Q30 · Week 8

Clock period 10 ns, clock-to-Q tcq = 2 ns, combinational delay 5 ns, setup tsu = 2 ns. The setup-time margin is:

  • A. 1 ns
  • B. 3 ns
  • C. 5 ns
  • D. −1 ns
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Setup margin = T − tcq − tcomb − tsu = 10 − 2 − 5 − 2 = 1 ns (positive = OK).

Q31 · Week 8

Shortest tcq = 1 ns, shortest combinational delay = 1 ns, hold time th = 1.5 ns. The hold-time margin is:

  • A. 0.5 ns
  • B. 2.5 ns
  • C. −0.5 ns
  • D. 1.5 ns
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Hold margin = tcq,min + tcomb,min − th = 1 + 1 − 1.5 = 0.5 ns. Notice it does not depend on the clock period, so slowing the clock cannot fix a hold violation.

Q32 · Week 8

A ring oscillator is made of 5 inverters, each with propagation delay 1 ns. Its period is:

  • A. 10 ns
  • B. 5 ns
  • C. 2.5 ns
  • D. 1 ns
Reveal answer
Answer: A

A signal must travel round the ring twice (once to flip each half-cycle): period = 2 × 5 × 1 ns = 10 ns (frequency 100 MHz). The number of inverters must be odd.

Q33 · Week 8

With the same figures as the setup question above (tcq 2, tcomb 5, tsu 2), the maximum clock frequency is about:

  • A. 111 MHz
  • B. 100 MHz
  • C. 200 MHz
  • D. 50 MHz
Reveal answer
Answer: A

Tmin = 2 + 5 + 2 = 9 ns ⇒ fmax = 1/9 ns = 111.1 MHz.

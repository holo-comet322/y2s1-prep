Weekly Routine — Outside-of-Class Study Blocks

Built from this semester's timetable · Monday–Friday only, weekends left completely free

How to read this

The grey rows are your fixed classes, taken straight off the timetable. Everything else is a suggested — not mandatory — block for self-study, tagged with which subject it's for and roughly what to do in it. Treat it as a default starting point: swap blocks around freely depending on what's actually due, what lecture just happened, or how you're feeling that day. The goal is just to make sure every subject gets touched regularly and nothing gets left to the night before a test.

The idea in one line Study time is scheduled the same way the classes already are — as fixed, recurring appointments with yourself — because a block that only exists as "I'll find time for it later" tends to lose out to whatever feels more urgent in the moment.

The principles behind this layout

  • Mornings before class are protected deep-work time on days that allow it (Mon/Tue/Wed/Fri) — your head is clearest then, so that's where the newer, harder subjects (IE2106, EE2005) go, since they need more raw problem-solving reps than IE2104 does right now.
  • Wednesday evening is deliberately light. Three back-to-back classes from 1330 to 1820 is already a full day — the evening slot is IE2104 review only (re-reading notes-boxes, no new problem sets), not another deep-work block.
  • IE2104 is in "maintenance mode," not because it matters less, but because it's already the most built-out — the 79-question exam-practice bank exists precisely so a rotation through a few questions a week keeps it fresh without needing a big dedicated block.
  • Friday has no scheduled classes at all, so it's the natural buffer/consolidation day — a morning pass over all three subjects' notes, then an afternoon flex block for whatever slipped during the week. If a week goes sideways, Friday is where it gets caught up, not the weekend.
  • Thursday flexes around the lab schedule — EE2005L and IE2104L only run on alternating weeks (7/12 and 8/11 respectively), so most Thursdays actually have a free morning; use it as a second deep-work slot on non-lab weeks.
  • Weekends are intentionally blank — as requested, this routine only covers Monday to Friday.
